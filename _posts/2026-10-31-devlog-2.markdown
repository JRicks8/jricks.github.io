## Prototype
I'm very happy to say that I finished a *very* basic prototype for the game. It has:
- Playable character
- Orbit camera, 3rd person
- Can zoom to 1st person
- Dirigible
- Items to carry and place, affected by physics
- Pickup & dropoff point

Which is conveniently exactly the things that I wanted to achieve last week, so I'm very happy with myself. I anticipated the prototype taking longer than my initial guess (1 week) so I gave myself twice the time. I suspect this will end up being a trend.

## Simulating Airships
Creating the physics for the ropes were particularly challenging. I felt that it was important that the airship felt physically *real*, in that it visibly reacted to turning, thrust, and even jumping on and off. This was going to take a little more than the run-of-the-mill physics joints that Godot provides, which are admittedly lackluster. They don't have adjustable attachment points, stiffness, damping, or really any configurability at all.

Then a plugin called [Verlet Rope 4](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/2308) came in handy! Since my project is using GDScript and not .NET, I had to re-write the codebase. I was a little hesitant at first, but I'm really glad I did. I'm much more confident with Godot plugins and GDScript in general. As a little bonus the plugin comes with rope rendering, which makes my box-and-capsule airship look pretty neat with ropes!

To fly, the airship applies a negative gravity scaling to the balloon, and any thrust/torque is also applied to the balloon. The ropes drag along the body and whatever is sitting on top.

## Simulating The Character
Unfortunately, the built-in character controller did not play nicely with the simulated airship. The character by default is **not** affected by any other physics bodies, which means the airship could not lift the player. This was a total dealbreaker, so I had to make my own solution.

I've made physics-based controllers in the past, although this case was a little different. I needed to make sure that the character's velocity properly matched the ground they were standing on, so I couldn't modify the velocity directly. I'd end up overriding the forces that other physics bodies in the world has on the player (the airship, for instance). I opted for a force-based approach. This controller has:
- Force application based on player input
- Turn assistance
- Slope detection
- Dynamic friction based on state (increased if no input)
- Inherited velocity of floor
- Different ground/air force multipliers

I'm happy with how it turned out.

## Flying Around
The ropes are a little bit too springy for my taste, but it's not altogether too bad. Overall, I'm happy with how this feels. There's definitely room for improvement and fine-tuning in the future.

I ended up having a rough landing...
{:refdef: style="text-align: center;"}
![Rough Landing](/assets/images/proto_landing.gif)
{: refdef}

Right now there's a lot of skill expression in navigating the airship properly, which is fun, but flying around feels pretty slow and clunky. It's certainly worth exploring making the dirigible a little more reactive. I am afraid of making the navigation *too* easy though. It would trivialize one of the core mechanics of the game! I don't want this to be a hardcore game with a steep learning curve, but I want there to *be* a learning curve. I'll have to find that balance somewhere down the line.

Something interesting that I noticed was that the cargo was actually causing the airship to be pulled down at the back, causing the whole thing to fly at a slant. This could be interesting in gameplay, where an imbalanced load of cargo can cause the balloon to go down!
